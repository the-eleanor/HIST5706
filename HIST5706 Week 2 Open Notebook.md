Connor, Kimberley G. “Unexpected Origins: Mapping Assisted Female Immigrants to New South Wales.” _Current Research in Digital History_ 9 (2026). [https://doi.org/10.31835/crdh.2026.01](https://doi.org/10.31835/crdh.2026.01).

-Project does not seem to have its own website, at least not linked to the article, but a quick internet search of the author's name brings up other academic works
-Dataset is openly available through Stanford Digital Repository
	-Author admits to resource limitations affecting how much of the data was used, but that the availability of the data means the work could be expanded upon in the future
-Re: FAIR and CARE: 
	-FAIR: Author states in conclusion that the data is available for others to use and FAIR compliant
	-CARE: The project does not mention use of indigenous data, but involves data regarding colonial immigration. Might the use of indigenous data and perspectives present a more full picture regarding the topic? Where are they in this situation?
-Topic has implications for gender studies in Australia (women were needed both for reproduction and employment as servants - says a lot about status of women at the time/place), as well as topics of marginalization like race, class/wealth
-Data being collected by imperial/colonial actors (ship logs, settler newspapers, etc) with certain views of gender, race, class means data is affected by these views
-New tools:
	-Recogito - collaborative tool for document/image/data annotation
	-Mapping software to show higher concentrations of data points
	-Would have to dig deeper into the use of these to get a grasp on how to use them but they seem like they are not dependent on anything but having the data available

Blevins, Cameron. “Women and Federal Officeholding in the Late Nineteenth-Century U.S.” _Current Research in Digital History_ 2 (2019). [https://doi.org/10.31835/crdh.2019.08](https://doi.org/10.31835/crdh.2019.08).

-Using multiple sources of data to analyze the topic (presidential appointments, state-level data, n-grams)
-Project does not have its own site
-Re: FAIR and CARE:
	-Not sure this is FAIR compliant - mentions of datasets (especially the presidential appointment and state-level data) compiled by specific individuals. The former was given to the author and the latter was scraped by the author off a site. Cannot see any links to where this data might be accessed.
	-CARE might not be directly relevant to this data, though it is definitely colonizer data and there is no information about whether Indigenous women were even allowed to be postmistresses. 
-Gender is being inferred through name with the first dataset. This seems like it could produce a lot of flawed information through miscounting people, but not sure there's a better way to assess the info if there's no clear gender marker in the data. Author acknowledges the inherently problematic aspect of this and that the data may undercount women.
-New(ish) concept: n-grams. Feels familiar, I'm pretty sure I knew what that was before, but it's definitely been a while.
	-relative frequency of word strings - lets you pull data that isn't explicitly measured in the database. Definitely could see this being a struggle to ensure every variation is counted or discounted as need be, but I can also see this being a good way to pull specific data out of sources that don't directly outline it.
